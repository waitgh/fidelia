Fidelia Ledger FAQ Open

Free for small churches

Offering and donor records,
without the spreadsheet.

Count Sunday’s offering, keep track of donors and pledges, and print year-end giving statements. It runs in your web browser with nothing to install, and your records stay in your church’s own Dropbox.

Try the demo Set up your church

The demo uses made-up data and needs no sign-up.

Only your church can read it

Records are encrypted in your browser before they are saved. The people who run this website cannot see your donors or your giving.

Kept in your own Dropbox

Your church file lives in a Dropbox folder that you own and share with your treasurers. A free Dropbox account is enough.

Nothing to install

Open the website on a laptop, and look things up on an iPhone. No servers, no updates to run, no IT person needed.

Sunday counting, done once

  • Enter each gift with its fund, cash or check number, and an optional designation.
  • See subtotals for every fund and payment method as you go.
  • Record attendance for adults and children.
  • Read Zelle and PayPal gifts from screenshots, or import a CSV.

A summary sheet for the counters to sign

One page per service: totals by fund and method, number of checks, attendance, and lines for Counted by and Verified by. Print it and file it with the deposit slip.

Year-end statements in minutes

  • Giving statements for every donor, as PDFs ready to print or send.
  • Summary and trend reports by fund, congregation and month.
  • Pledges and how much of each has been given.

Built for a team of volunteers

  • Admins, bookkeepers and view-only members, each with their own password.
  • Invite a new treasurer with a one-time code.
  • Every change is recorded in an activity log, and earlier versions can be restored.
  • A printed recovery key, so the church never loses access.

Made by a church volunteer

Fidelia started as the bookkeeping tool for one church’s offering counters and treasurers. It is now offered to other churches, free for small churches and other noncommercial use. The source code is public, so anyone can check how it protects your records.
